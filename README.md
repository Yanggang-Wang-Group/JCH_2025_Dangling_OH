# JCH_2025_Dangling_OH

Raw data for "Universal Dangling OH Bonds at Electrochemically Tunable Aromatic-Water Interfaces" published in CCS Chemistry.

https://doi.org/10.31635/ccschem.026.202607991

Understanding the structure of interfacial water is fundamental to diverse chemical and catalytic processes, yet its organization around ubiquitous hydrophobic molecules, particularly under electrochemical control, remains elusive. Using ab initio molecular dynamics simulations, we uncover a universal structural signature at hydrophobic interfaces: dangling OH bonds (OH stretch ∼3700 cm−1) across diverse aromatic molecules (benzaldehyde, furan, benzene, naphthalene). Crucially, the population of these aromatic dangling OH bonds increase with the application of negative electrode potentials. Thermodynamic and dynamic lifetime analyses corroborate this trend and reveal that π–H interactions help stabilize the dangling O–H configurations under negatively charged interfaces. Importantly, dangling water could also act as an effective proton donor compared with surface adsorbed *H, underscoring its critical role in altering interfacial proton transfer pathways. This work redefines our understanding of water organization near hydrophobic adsorbates and reveals a previously overlooked, potential-driven proton-transfer pathway that is critical for electrocatalytic processes such as aromatic hydrogenation.
